On 15 November 2025 I got on stage at [Sword AI Summit](https://aisummit.swordhealth.com/) in Porto and gave a talk called **Real AI Workflows for Software Engineers**.

It was, genuinely, one of the coolest things I have done.

I am a software engineer in Berlin. Most days I build energy and clean-tech systems. Standing in a room of people who actually *build* AI — and talking about how I use it to ship software — sat well outside my comfort zone. That was the point. I wanted the nerves. I wanted the curiosity. I wanted to see what would happen if I stopped watching this stuff from a tab and showed up.

The day itself was a rush. The venue, the talks, the hallway conversations, the feeling of being in the middle of it instead of catching the recording later. I learned more in one Saturday than I had in months of quiet tinkering. I walked out wanting to do it again.

It also lit a fire I had been politely ignoring.

I have had drafts sitting around for years — notes, half-finished guides, workflows I keep explaining to friends over coffee. Publishing always felt like a "personal brand" thing other people did. A bit loud. A bit not-me. After that stage, the opposite felt true. If I am going to keep doing this — talks, workflows, building in public — I need a place that is mine. Not a company channel. Not a thread that disappears in two days. A blog I can stand behind.

So this is it.

Views here are my own. Nothing I write represents my employer or anyone I work with.

What you will find: how I actually work with AI as a software engineer, energy and agents, homelab experiments, side projects, and the notes I wish I had written down the first time. Some of it will be polished. Some of it will be me figuring it out in the open.

I want to get better at this — writing, speaking, showing up. That starts here.

Hello world. Let's see where this goes.
