Every personal site needs a first post. Mine sat as a draft long enough that the joke started writing itself. So: hello world — for real this time.

## Who I am

I'm Pedro — a software engineer in Berlin. Day job is energy and clean-tech: systems that help grids and people behave a little more intelligently. After hours I keep a home lab, take notes on books I actually finish, and tinker with AI tooling until something useful sticks. I ride, I lift when I can, and I read more than I publish.

## What this place is for

This is my writing desk, not a product blog. Markdown in git, push to publish, no CMS theatre. You'll mostly see:

- **Energy × agents** — spot prices, load shifting, skills that can *act* (with dry-run defaults, because cheap hours shouldn't mean chaos)
- **Homelab and self-hosted AI** — always-on boxes, private mesh, gateways that keep memory and tools somewhere I own
- **How I build** — coding setups, multi-agent skills, the odd side project that got weird enough to ship

Study notes and longer guides live elsewhere on the site. The blog is for the stuff I want to stand behind in public.

## What this is not

Not a company channel. Views here are mine. Nothing I write represents my employer or anyone I work with.

## Why now

I've been writing into private folders for years — half-finished guides, lab notes, ideas that never left the house. The site is live, the pipeline works, and "I'll publish when it's perfect" has expired as an excuse.

Next up: the homelab journey — why I pulled interesting work back onto hardware I can touch. After that: OpenClaw on the lab, energy skills, and the AI coding setup that ties the day job to the evening tinkering.

Hello world. Let's see where this goes.
